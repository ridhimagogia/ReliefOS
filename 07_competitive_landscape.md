# 07. Competitive Landscape

**Status: from background knowledge [B]. Not yet verified. Step 1b will verify each row with sources before it goes on a slide.**

| Tool / body | What it does | Gap vs ReliefOS |
|---|---|---|
| Ushahidi | Crowd-sourced crisis reporting and mapping | Reports and maps; does not match supply to demand or plan transport |
| Sahana Eden | Open-source disaster management (inventory, shelters, people) | Heavy, form-driven; limited AI intake and optimisation |
| UN OCHA / Logistics Cluster tools, HDX | Coordination, shared data | Coordination layer for big agencies; not an operational matcher for local NGOs |
| WFP / humanitarian supply chain systems | Internal logistics for large agencies | Not shared with small NGOs/donors |
| India Disaster Resource Network (IDRN) | Government registry of resources | Registry; not live demand matching [B] |
| Government state portals (e.g., Kerala Rescue 2019) | Public needs lists and collection centres | Manual updates; state-specific |
| Social media and chat groups (WhatsApp, Telegram) | What people actually use | Unstructured, no prioritisation. Telegram channels used for relief in Pakistan 2025 [V] |
| Google Crisis Response / Person Finder | Information and alerts | Not a logistics matcher |
| Academic prototypes | Models for prioritised demand points (Rivera-Royero 2016) | Papers, not usable products |

**Positioning sentence for slides (draft):** "Today's tools map the crisis or register resources. ReliefOS closes the loop: messy report, structured demand, ranked move."
